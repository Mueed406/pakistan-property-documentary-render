## Visual language
- Cinematic survival-horror, dim industrial hallways, glowing red emergency indicators, metal/grime PBR, textured plastic toys, volumetric lighting, long shadows, controlled perspective and subtle cinematic grain.
- No documentary text blocks, papers, date stamps, kinetic infographics or generic web dashboard UI.
- Strong camera language: stalking dolly, low-angle chase, camera bob with physical movement, close focus pulls, subtle motion blur. Reserve intense glitch and whip movement for story-motivated moments.
- Fan characters are conditional use under Mob Entertainment policy; must be visibly recognisable but never look like or claim to be an official trailer.
- Movie footage must be gameplay **captured by creator or explicitly approved**, not random creator footage scraped from YouTube.

## Required 3D source per important shot
- Every hero 3D character: coherent skeletal joints/weights, eye gaze controller, body language, contact and collision checks; lip-sync only if actual speaking face visible.
- GrabPack equivalent: motion and line/cable endpoints anchored to correct joints and world-space switch targets; no teleporting hand or intersecting walls.
- All factory/corridor models: continuous environment coordinate system, correct scale, PBR albedo/normal/roughness, illumination and shadow/culling proof.
- Temporal continuity: consistent lights, doll size, props, camera side and physical position across cuts.
- In suspense shots, blocking and lighting should make the action readable on a phone display.

## Audio acts
- English hero breath/dialogue (original performance).
- One eerie tape voice, one distinct Poppy-like role only if lawful to portray; do **not** clone an official actor's voice without authorization.
- Human audition: acting > shouted robotic narration. Separate stems for hero, tape, Poppy, footsteps, ambience, music, impacts.
- English closed captions only when narratively useful, aligned to *actual recorded voice*, not made-up dialogue timing.
